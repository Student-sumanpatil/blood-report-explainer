# Lipid Panel (Cholesterol Profile) Medical Reference

## Metadata
- **Source**: National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI / NIH) & MedlinePlus
- **Topic URL**: https://medlineplus.gov/cholesterollevelsandtests.html
- **Last Verified**: 2025

---

### 1. Total Cholesterol
- **What it is**: Total cholesterol represents the overall amount of cholesterol in your blood, including both LDL (low-density lipoprotein) and HDL (high-density lipoprotein).
- **Target Desirable Range**: Less than 200 mg/dL (5.18 mmol/L)
- **Borderline High**: 200 to 239 mg/dL
- **High**: 240 mg/dL and above
- **Interpretation Context**: Total cholesterol is an overview metric; clinicians evaluate it in conjunction with HDL, LDL, and individual cardiovascular risk factors rather than in isolation.

---

### 2. LDL Cholesterol ("Bad" Cholesterol)
- **What it is**: Low-density lipoprotein carries cholesterol to tissues. When excessive, it can build up as plaque inside arterial walls (atherosclerosis).
- **Target Desirable Range**: Less than 100 mg/dL (for higher cardiovascular risk individuals, targets may be under 70 mg/dL)
- **Borderline High**: 100 to 129 mg/dL
- **High**: 130 to 159 mg/dL
- **Very High**: 160 mg/dL and above
- **What Elevated Levels May Indicate**: Diets high in saturated/trans fats, lack of aerobic exercise, genetic factors (familial hypercholesterolemia), or metabolic imbalances.
- **Questions for Your Doctor**:
  - What is my target LDL based on my personal and family heart health history?
  - What lifestyle or dietary modifications do you recommend first?

---

### 3. HDL Cholesterol ("Good" Cholesterol)
- **What it is**: High-density lipoprotein absorbs cholesterol from tissues and the bloodstream, carrying it back to the liver for excretion.
- **Target Desirable Range**: 
  - Males: 40 mg/dL or higher (optimal > 50 mg/dL)
  - Females: 50 mg/dL or higher (optimal > 60 mg/dL)
- **What Low Levels May Indicate**: Lower HDL is associated with an elevated risk of cardiovascular events. Factors lowering HDL include smoking, sedentary lifestyle, and insulin resistance.
- **Questions for Your Doctor**:
  - Are there specific aerobic exercises or nutritional habits to help improve my HDL?

---

### 4. Triglycerides
- **What it is**: Triglycerides are the most common type of fat stored in the body, derived from unused calories, sugars, and alcohol.
- **Target Desirable Range**: Less than 150 mg/dL
- **Borderline High**: 150 to 199 mg/dL
- **High**: 200 to 499 mg/dL
- **Very High / Critical Alert**: 500 mg/dL and above (elevated risk of acute pancreatitis)
- **What Elevated Levels May Indicate**: High refined carbohydrate or sugar intake, alcohol consumption, poorly controlled blood sugar, or thyroid sluggishness.
- **Questions for Your Doctor**:
  - Was this test properly fasted (9-12 hours)?
  - How do dietary sugars or alcohol influence my triglyceride levels?
