# Thyroid Function Tests Medical Reference

## Metadata
- **Source**: American Thyroid Association (ATA) / MedlinePlus (NIH)
- **Topic URL**: https://medlineplus.gov/thyroidtests.html
- **Last Verified**: 2025

---

### 1. Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone (TSH / Thyrotropin)
- **What it is**: Produced by the pituitary gland in the brain, TSH directs the thyroid gland to produce hormones T3 and T4. It operates on a reverse feedback loop: when thyroid hormone is low, the pituitary pumps more TSH; when thyroid hormone is high, TSH drops.
- **Typical Reference Range**: 0.40 to 4.50 µIU/mL (or mIU/L)
- **What High Levels May Indicate (Hypothyroidism)**: An elevated TSH indicates the thyroid gland is sluggish or underactive, prompting the pituitary to work harder. Common symptoms include unexplained fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain, constipation, and dry skin.
- **What Low Levels May Indicate (Hyperthyroidism)**: A suppressed or low TSH indicates the thyroid gland is overproducing hormones. Common signs include unexplained weight loss, rapid heart rate, nervousness, heat intolerance, and insomnia.
- **Questions for Your Doctor**:
  - Should we also measure Free T3, Free T4, and anti-TPO thyroid antibodies?
  - Was this test influenced by biotin supplements or morning medication timing?

---

### 2. Free Thyroxine (Free T4 / FT4)
- **What it is**: The active, unbound form of thyroxine circulating in the blood, representing hormone readily available for cells.
- **Typical Reference Range**: 0.8 to 1.8 ng/dL (10 to 23 pmol/L)
- **Clinical Significance**: Evaluated in combination with TSH. High TSH + Low Free T4 indicates primary overt hypothyroidism. Low TSH + High Free T4 indicates overt hyperthyroidism.

---

### 3. Free Triiodothyronine (Free T3 / FT3)
- **What it is**: The most metabolically active thyroid hormone, largely converted from T4 in peripheral tissues (liver, kidneys).
- **Typical Reference Range**: 2.3 to 4.2 pg/mL
- **Clinical Significance**: Primarily helpful when hyperthyroidism is suspected or when TSH is suppressed while Free T4 is normal (T3 thyrotoxicosis).
