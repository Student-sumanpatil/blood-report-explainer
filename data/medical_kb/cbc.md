# Complete Blood Count (CBC) Medical Reference

## Metadata
- **Source**: U.S. National Library of Medicine (MedlinePlus / NIH)
- **Topic URL**: https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/complete-blood-count/
- **Last Verified**: 2025

---

### 1. Hemoglobin (Hb / HGB)
- **What it is**: Hemoglobin is the iron-rich protein in red blood cells that transports oxygen from your lungs to tissues throughout your body.
- **Typical Reference Range**:
  - Adult Males: 13.8 to 17.2 g/dL
  - Adult Females: 12.1 to 15.1 g/dL
- **What Low Levels May Indicate**: Low hemoglobin indicates that blood oxygen delivery is reduced. Common non-diagnostic factors include nutritional deficiencies (iron, vitamin B12, or folate), blood loss (such as heavy menstrual bleeding or gastrointestinal bleeding), or chronic medical conditions.
- **What High Levels May Indicate**: Elevated hemoglobin can occur when the body requires higher oxygen carrying capacity, such as living at high altitudes, chronic smoking, dehydration (relative hemoconcentration), or specific bone marrow conditions.
- **Questions for Your Doctor**:
  - Is my hemoglobin value related to my iron intake or recent fatigue?
  - Do I need follow-up ferritin or iron saturation tests?

---

### 2. White Blood Cell Count (WBC / Leukocytes)
- **What it is**: White blood cells are essential components of the immune system that defend the body against infections, foreign substances, and cellular damage.
- **Typical Reference Range**: 4,000 to 11,000 cells/µL (or 4.0 to 11.0 x 10^9/L)
- **What Low Levels May Indicate (Leukopenia)**: Reduced immune cell counts, which can be associated with viral infections, autoimmune conditions, bone marrow issues, or specific medications.
- **What High Levels May Indicate (Leukocytosis)**: Elevated levels are a typical physiological response to active infection (bacterial or viral), acute physical stress, systemic inflammation, allergic reactions, or tissue injury.
- **Questions for Your Doctor**:
  - Could recent stress, allergy, or a minor infection explain this count?
  - Should we repeat the test in a few weeks to check if it returns to baseline?

---

### 3. Platelet Count (PLT / Thrombocytes)
- **What it is**: Platelets are cell fragments crucial for normal blood clotting and stopping bleeding from injured blood vessels.
- **Typical Reference Range**: 150,000 to 450,000 /µL
- **What Low Levels May Indicate (Thrombocytopenia)**: Increased risk of bruising or bleeding. May be caused by viral infections (such as dengue or viral fevers), spleen enlargement, autoimmune destruction, or medications.
  - *Critical Alert*: Values below 30,000 /µL represent a high bleeding risk and require prompt medical evaluation.
- **What High Levels May Indicate (Thrombocytosis)**: Elevated platelets can be a secondary reaction to inflammation, iron deficiency, recent surgery, or primary marrow overproduction.
- **Questions for Your Doctor**:
  - Should I avoid blood-thinning over-the-counter pain relievers (like aspirin/ibuprofen)?
  - Are there any signs of bleeding or bruising I should monitor?

---

### 4. Red Blood Cell Count (RBC) & Hematocrit (HCT)
- **What it is**: RBC count measures the actual number of oxygen-carrying cells, while Hematocrit measures the proportion of total blood volume composed of red blood cells.
- **Typical Reference Range**:
  - RBC: 4.5 to 5.9 million/µL (males), 4.1 to 5.1 million/µL (females)
  - Hematocrit: 41% to 50% (males), 36% to 48% (females)
- **Interpretation Context**: RBC and Hematocrit typically move in parallel with Hemoglobin.
