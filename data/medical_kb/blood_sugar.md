# Blood Sugar & Glycemic Control Medical Reference

## Metadata
- **Source**: American Diabetes Association (ADA) / MedlinePlus (NIH)
- **Topic URL**: https://medlineplus.gov/bloodsugar.html
- **Last Verified**: 2025

---

### 1. Fasting Blood Glucose (Fasting Blood Sugar / FBS)
- **What it is**: Measures circulating glucose in blood after an overnight fast of at least 8 to 10 hours.
- **Reference Categories**:
  - **Normal**: 70 to 99 mg/dL
  - **Impaired Fasting Glucose (Pre-diabetic Range)**: 100 to 125 mg/dL
  - **Elevated (Diagnostic Threshold for Diabetes)**: 126 mg/dL or higher on two separate occasions
- **Critical Alerts**:
  - Below 54 mg/dL: Severe hypoglycemia (requires immediate fast-acting carbohydrate).
  - Above 300 mg/dL: Severe hyperglycemia (consult physician immediately).
- **Questions for Your Doctor**:
  - Was my fast adequate prior to the blood draw?
  - Should we evaluate an HbA1c test to see a longer-term average?

---

### 2. Postprandial Blood Glucose (PPBS / 2-Hour Glucose)
- **What it is**: Measures glucose levels exactly 2 hours after the start of a meal or standardized oral glucose tolerance drink.
- **Reference Categories**:
  - **Normal**: Less than 140 mg/dL
  - **Impaired Glucose Tolerance**: 140 to 199 mg/dL
  - **Elevated**: 200 mg/dL or higher

---

### 3. Glycated Hemoglobin (HbA1c / Hemoglobin A1c)
- **What it is**: Measures the percentage of hemoglobin proteins coated with glucose, reflecting the average blood sugar level over the preceding 2 to 3 months (the lifespan of a red blood cell).
- **Reference Categories**:
  - **Normal**: Below 5.7%
  - **Pre-diabetes Range**: 5.7% to 6.4%
  - **Diabetes Range**: 6.5% or higher
- **Key Advantages**: Does not require fasting on the day of the test; unaffected by day-to-day acute stress or single meals.
- **Questions for Your Doctor**:
  - What is a realistic 3-month HbA1c goal for me?
  - How frequently should I retest my HbA1c?
